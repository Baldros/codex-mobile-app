Compile Codex Mobile from scratch

An Android client that operates a local Codex CLI session on your desktop, talking to her through a secure SSH tunnel — including on mobile networks. This guide takes you from the empty environment to the installed APK, without opening anything from your machine for the internet.

What will you build

The mobile app is just a operation customer. All power — the Codex CLI, OpenAI login, workspace, and permissions — remains in desktop, which is the trust boundary. The cell phone talks to a Codex Bridge location through an SSH tunnel, and the only service reachable from outside is SSH.

Conventions in this guide

The commands use placeholders and environment variables instead of actual values. Always replace the sections between < > for their own values ​​and never write secrets directly to versioned files.

PlaceholderMeans
<PROJECT_ROOT>Folder where you cloned the app repository.
<YOUR_USER>Your development Windows user.
<SSH_USER>Dedicated and unprivileged user, created just for the tunnel (e.g.: codex_ssh).
<BRIDGE_PORT>door loopback where Codex Bridge listens. Defined by you, it remains only in 127.0.0.1.
<LOCAL_FORWARD_PORT>door loopback on your cell phone to the local end of the tunnel.
<SSH_ENDPOINT>How does the cell phone reach the desktop's sshd (LAN, IPv6 or VPN).
Principle that guides everything here

The Codex Bridge is stuck to the loopback (127.0.0.1) and never is published on the network. It is only SSH that crosses the internet. That's why the guide avoids exposing ports, IPs or paths on your machine — and recommends that you do the same.

Common Terminal × Administrator

Good news: the build itself does not require administrator. Node, npm, Expo, Gradle and adb run in a common PowerShell, as long as the variables and PATH are already configured. What do you need Administrator is the one-time system preparation.
